The Denver problem

Why paint fails faster in Denver than almost anywhere else

A paint job that lasts twelve years on the Gulf Coast can start chalking here in five. That is not a materials problem. It is an altitude problem, and it changes how the work has to be done.

The atmosphere is the filter. At a mile up there is measurably less of it overhead, so more ultraviolet light reaches the south and west faces of your house every single day.

Ultraviolet light is what actually destroys paint

UV breaks down the resin binder that holds pigment to your siding. When the binder fails you get chalking, fading and eventually peeling. The Environmental Protection Agency reports that UV radiation increases roughly two percent for every 1,000 feet of elevation gain. Denver sits at 5,280 feet.

That effect compounds. Colorado's UV Index routinely reaches the "very high" and "extreme" ranges on the EPA scale, including on cool days and partly cloudy days when nobody thinks about sun exposure. Your north wall and your south wall are living in two different climates.

A south-facing wall in Denver takes a beating your north-facing wall never sees. Painting them identically, with the same product and the same number of coats, is why so many repaints look uneven after four years.

Then the freeze-thaw cycle finishes the job

Denver swings hard. It is common to see a forty degree temperature change inside a single day in spring and fall. Every one of those swings makes your siding expand and contract. Moisture that got into a hairline crack freezes, expands, and pushes the coating off the substrate from underneath.

This is why caulk inspection matters more here than in most markets, and why we check every joint around windows, doors and trim before a brush ever comes out.

And Colorado sits in Hail Alley

Colorado, Nebraska and Wyoming form the region the National Weather Service calls Hail Alley, the most hail-prone area in the country. Hail does not just dent gutters. It chips coating off the high side of lap siding and drives moisture into the exposed wood underneath, where it sits and rots quietly for a season before anyone notices.

Sources: U.S. Environmental Protection Agency UV Index; NOAA National Severe Storms Laboratory.

How it works

Five steps, and four of them happen before we open a can

Ask any painter what separates a good job from a bad one and they will tell you the same thing. It is not the paint. It is what happened to the surface before the paint got there.

Prep is the single largest labor line on a proper exterior estimate. If a bid comes in dramatically under everyone else, prep is almost always where the hours were removed.

What "prep" actually means on our estimates

It is a word every painter uses and almost nobody itemizes. Here is what it covers on a Denver exterior:

Pressure washing. Not just dirt. Denver's wind drives fine dust into every horizontal surface, and that dust holds moisture against your siding. Chalked coating has to come off too, or the new paint bonds to loose powder instead of wood.

Scraping and feathering. Failed paint gets scraped back to a sound edge, then the transition gets sanded so it disappears under the new coat instead of telegraphing through it.

Wood repair. Rotted trim, fascia and sill gets replaced before coating. Painting over rot hides it for one season and costs more to fix the next.

Caulking. Every joint where two materials meet gets inspected. Failed caulk is the main way water gets behind a coating during freeze-thaw.

Priming. Bare wood, patched areas and severe color changes get primed. Skipping primer on bare substrate is the fastest way to a peel in three years.

Products we use, and why

We work primarily with Sherwin-Williams and Benjamin Moore. Both make exterior lines with the UV inhibitors and flexible binders that altitude and freeze-thaw demand.

Flexibility matters more here than most homeowners realize. A brittle coating cracks when siding expands on a seventy degree afternoon and contracts on a thirty degree night. A flexible acrylic moves with it.

If you want to preview colors before we meet, Sherwin-Williams runs a free color visualizer where you can upload a photo of your own house and try combinations on it. Homeowners who come to the estimate with two or three favorites make the whole process faster.

Timing

When to paint the outside of a house in Denver

Colorado gives you a real exterior season and a real off-season, plus some surprisingly good shoulder weeks that most homeowners never think to use.

Most exterior products need surface temperatures to stay above roughly 50°F through cure, though some modern acrylics are rated lower. The limiting factor in Denver is usually the overnight low, not the daytime high.

May through September is the safe window

Long days, warm surfaces and reliable overnight lows. The one thing to plan around is the afternoon thunderstorm pattern that sets up in July and August. Fresh coating and a sudden downpour do not mix, so summer work means watching the radar and painting the right elevation at the right hour.

Late April and October are underrated

Cooler air, lower UV and far less competition for calendar space. Cooler surfaces also mean a longer wet edge, which on a large wall produces a more uniform finish than painting in ninety degree afternoon sun. If your schedule is flexible, the shoulder weeks are often the better job.

November through March is interior season

Exterior work becomes weather dependent, but this is the right time for interiors, cabinets and commercial spaces. It is also when scheduling is easiest. If you want exterior work done first thing in spring, winter is when to get on the calendar.

How to tell your exterior is due

Walk the perimeter on a bright day and look for these. Any two together means it is time to get a quote.

  • ● Chalking. Rub the siding with a dark cloth. If white powder comes off, the binder has broken down under UV.
  • ● Uneven fade. Compare the south wall to the north wall. A visible difference means the sun-exposed side is near end of life.
  • ● Caulk pulling away. Check around windows, doors and trim joints. Gaps are how water gets behind the coating.
  • ● Peeling or blistering. Usually a moisture problem underneath, not just a coating problem. Worth diagnosing before repainting.
  • ● Exposed bare wood. Every week bare wood sits open, it takes on moisture. This one is urgent.
  • ● Hail chipping on lap edges. Common in the metro after a storm season and easy to miss from the ground.
The research

What paint actually does to a home's sale price

Paint is one of the few home improvements with published, large-sample data behind it. Not contractor opinion. Actual analysis of what homes sold for.

Zillow analyzed 32,000 sold listings

Zillow's Paint Colors Analysis reviewed more than 32,000 photos from listings that sold across the country, controlling for square footage, home age, transaction date and location. The findings on exteriors were direct.

Homes with exteriors in "greige," a blend of light gray and beige, sold for $3,496 more than comparable homes painted medium brown or finished in tan stucco. Front doors in dark navy through slate gray brought roughly $1,514 more at closing.

A later expansion of that work analyzed more than 135,000 listing photos from sales between 2010 and 2018.

The 2026 update found the same pattern

Zillow's more recent research surveyed thousands of home buyers on how interior paint color affected their interest and their offer price. Sage green was the only shade to land in the top tier in every room tested. On the other end, a bold red bathroom drew offers roughly $7,971 lower than the same room in white, the single largest negative effect they measured.

Source: Zillow Paint Colors Analysis and Zillow buyer research. Figures are national averages, not Denver-specific, and reflect correlation across large samples rather than a guarantee on any individual home.

The practical read for Denver: neutral, light exteriors both perform better at resale and hold up better under high-altitude UV. Dark colors absorb more heat, which puts more thermal stress on siding at 5,280 feet.

How to compare three bids properly

Get three. Everyone says it and it is still right. But compare them on structure, not on the bottom line.

Look at how prep is described. A bid that says "prep and paint" is hiding the largest variable in the job. A bid that itemizes washing, scraping, caulking, priming and repair is telling you what you are buying.

Ask how many coats, and on what. Two coats on the body but one on the trim is a common way to shave a bid.

Ask what product line, specifically. "Sherwin-Williams" is a company. The product line inside it is what determines UV performance.

Ask who is actually on site. Some companies subcontract everything. That is not automatically bad, but you should know before, not after.

Ask about the warranty in writing. Verbal warranties are worth what they cost.

Why the cheapest bid usually costs more

Painting is a labor business. Material is a minority of the cost on most residential jobs. When a bid comes in dramatically low, the hours came out of somewhere, and it is almost always prep.

The result is a job that looks identical for about eighteen months. Then the parts that were not properly prepped start to fail, and because the failure is patchy rather than uniform, the fix is a full repaint rather than a touch-up.

Paying twice in five years is more expensive than paying once in twelve. That is the entire economic argument for prep, and it is why our estimates itemize it.

The reviews above mention prep repeatedly and without prompting. That is not an accident of wording. It is what homeowners notice when it is done properly.

Selling, buying or refinancing

Painting on a real estate timeline

A large share of the painting work in Denver happens against a deadline someone else set. Listing dates, closing dates, inspection objections and lease turnovers all come with a clock attached.

Before you list

Exterior paint is the first thing a buyer sees and the first thing that shows up in listing photos. The Zillow research above found measurable differences in sale price tied to exterior color alone, and that is before accounting for the impression that peeling or chalking makes on a buyer walking up the driveway.

If you are listing, the two highest-return moves are usually a neutral exterior refresh and cabinet refinishing. Both photograph extremely well and both cost far less than the kitchen or siding replacement a buyer might otherwise mentally deduct from their offer.

Timing matters. Painting after the listing photos are taken defeats the purpose. We can work backward from your photo date.

Inspection objections

Peeling exterior paint and exposed bare wood show up on inspection reports constantly in Denver, and they can hold up an FHA or VA loan specifically. If you are under contract and an objection landed on your desk with a deadline attached, tell us that when you call. Those jobs get scoped differently because the goal is clearing the objection, not a full cosmetic repaint.

After you buy

The easiest time to paint the interior of a house is the window between closing and moving in. No furniture to move, no rooms to work around, no living in a construction zone. If you are closing on a Denver home and want the interior done before the truck arrives, that window is usually tight but very workable if we know about it early.

Realtors, property managers and HOA boards

If you manage property or list homes for a living, you need three things from a painting contractor: predictable scheduling, work that survives a walkthrough, and someone who answers the phone. We are set up for repeat relationships rather than one-off transactions, and we are happy to walk a property and provide scoped estimates for a client before they have committed to anything.

Working against a closing date? Say so on the first call. It changes how we sequence the crew, and it is much easier to plan for at the start than to accommodate halfway through.

Questions

Denver painting questions, answered straight

How much does it cost to paint a house in Denver?

Price depends on square footage, number of stories, siding material, the condition of the existing coating, how many colors you want and how much prep the surface needs. Condition is the largest swing factor by a wide margin. A house with sound existing paint needs a wash and two coats. A house with widespread failure needs scraping, sanding, rot repair and priming first, which can double the labor. We provide free written, itemized estimates so you can see exactly what you are paying for rather than a single lump number.

What is the best time of year to paint a house exterior in Denver?

May through September is the most reliable window. Late April and October are excellent and often overlooked, with cooler surfaces that hold a longer wet edge and produce a more uniform finish than painting in direct afternoon sun. The limiting factor in Denver is usually the overnight low rather than the daytime high, since most exterior products need surface temperatures above roughly 50°F through cure. November through March is the right time for interior, cabinet and commercial work.

Why does exterior paint fail faster in Colorado?

Three reasons stacked on top of each other. First, altitude. The EPA reports UV radiation increases about two percent per 1,000 feet of elevation, and Denver sits at 5,280 feet. UV breaks down the resin binder that holds pigment to the siding. Second, freeze-thaw. Denver's daily temperature swings make siding expand and contract constantly, and moisture in a hairline crack freezes and pushes coating off from underneath. Third, hail. Colorado sits in the region the National Weather Service calls Hail Alley, and hail chips coating off lap siding edges, letting moisture into the wood.

How long should a paint job last in Denver?

Quality exterior work with proper prep generally holds up well for many years, but the honest answer is that it varies more here than in most markets. South and west facing walls take substantially more UV than north facing walls and will show wear first. Horizontal surfaces like decks and railings need attention on a much shorter cycle than vertical walls. The two biggest determinants are the quality of the prep and whether a premium exterior product was used.

Yes. Our crew handles moving, covering and repositioning larger furniture. Larger pieces get shifted to the center of the room and fully draped, and floors are protected throughout. We do ask that you clear smaller items from shelves, countertops and windowsills before the first day.

Can you paint kitchen cabinets instead of replacing them?

In most cases yes, and it is one of the highest-impact dollars in a kitchen. Refinishing costs a fraction of replacement and takes days rather than weeks. Doors and drawers come off and get sprayed for a factory-smooth finish, hardware is removed and reinstalled, and boxes are finished in place. Cabinets that are structurally sound but dated are ideal candidates.

How do I compare painting quotes fairly?

Compare the structure of the bids, not just the totals. Look at how prep is described, since that is the largest variable and the line most often cut to win a job. Ask who will physically be on site. And get the warranty in writing. If one bid is dramatically lower than the others, the hours came out of somewhere, and it is almost always prep.
